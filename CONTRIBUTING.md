# Contributing to Unfiltered OT Security

Thank you for helping improve this global OT and ICS security knowledge base. Contributions must be accurate, attributable, safe for industrial operations, and useful to practitioners.

## Contribution principles

1. Submit original explanations. Do not copy copyrighted standards, paid reports, vendor manuals, or books.
2. Cite primary sources wherever possible: regulators, standards bodies, national CERTs, product security advisories, peer-reviewed research, and recognized technical organizations.
3. State jurisdiction, industry, system type, edition, publication date, effective date, and applicability where relevant.
4. Distinguish binding law, regulatory requirements, mandatory standards, contractual obligations, voluntary frameworks, and recommendations.
5. Never imply that a control mapping establishes legal compliance or certification.
6. Treat safety, process availability, environmental impact, and recovery as first-class considerations.
7. Keep demonstrations in isolated, authorized labs. Do not publish instructions that enable disruption of live industrial processes.
8. Do not include credentials, private keys, customer data, sensitive network diagrams, personal information, or non-public vulnerability details.

## Required structure for substantial technical documents

- Title and document status
- Purpose and scope
- Audience and prerequisites
- Terminology and assumptions
- Architecture or process context
- Assets, trust boundaries, interfaces, and data flows
- Threats, failure modes, and operational consequences
- Security requirements and implementation guidance
- Monitoring, detection, response, and recovery
- Verification approach and evidence examples
- Safety and operational constraints
- Standards and jurisdictional references
- Limitations and unresolved questions
- Revision history and last reviewed date

## Source quality

Prefer official publications and product advisories. For each source record title, publisher, canonical URL, publication or update date, version, access date, jurisdiction, and the claims it supports. Secondary sources may provide context but should not replace primary evidence for regulatory claims.

## Pull request checklist

- [ ] Scope and intended audience are clear.
- [ ] Claims are supported by cited sources.
- [ ] Versions and dates are checked.
- [ ] Mandatory requirements are distinguished from guidance.
- [ ] No unsupported compliance equivalence is claimed.
- [ ] Operational safety and availability are considered.
- [ ] No secrets or sensitive site information are included.
- [ ] Links work and diagrams are readable.
- [ ] Content uses the project terminology and Markdown conventions.

## Review

Regulatory, safety, vulnerability, and incident-response material requires careful review. Maintainers may request changes, defer publication pending verification, or remove material that creates safety, legal, privacy, or security concerns.
