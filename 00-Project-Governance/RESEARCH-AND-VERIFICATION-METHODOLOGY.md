# Research and Verification Methodology

## Objective

Ensure the knowledge base is useful without overstating certainty, currency, regulatory authority, or technical applicability.

## Research workflow

1. Define the question, target system, sector, jurisdiction, and intended decision.
2. Identify primary sources and the version relevant to the question.
3. Capture source metadata and access date.
4. Extract only claims supported by the source.
5. Compare with relevant technical standards, vendor guidance, and credible research.
6. Separate source facts from project interpretation and recommendations.
7. Identify conflicts, gaps, scope exclusions, and unknowns.
8. Have a qualified reviewer assess high-impact material.
9. Publish with status, review date, and change history.
10. Revisit when a source or applicability condition changes.

## Evidence grading

- **A: Primary authoritative evidence.** Official regulator, law, standards body, national CERT, vendor advisory, or original research.
- **B: Strong secondary evidence.** Recognized technical body, peer-reviewed review, or established research organization.
- **C: Contextual evidence.** Reputable industry publication or practitioner analysis.
- **D: Unverified lead.** Community post, social media, unattributed claim, or incomplete reference.

Grade each claim, not merely the document. D-grade material must not support a compliance conclusion or high-impact recommendation.

## Claim types

- **Source fact:** Explicitly stated by the cited source.
- **Project interpretation:** Analysis derived from one or more sources.
- **Recommendation:** Proposed action requiring local engineering and risk validation.
- **Assumption:** A condition used for an example or model.
- **Unknown:** Not established by available evidence.

## Conflict handling

Do not silently choose between conflicting sources. Record the source dates, scope, authority, and reason for the selected interpretation. For regulatory conflicts, seek the regulator's current official publication or legal interpretation.

## Regulatory verification

Check the official issuing authority, legal instrument or control document, applicability scope, covered entity definition, sector, territory, effective date, amendments, exemptions, and supersession. If any critical field is unknown, label the entry as requiring verification.

## Technical verification

Confirm vendor and product family, firmware or software version, deployment model, protocol profile, prerequisites, and operational constraints. Distinguish a protocol capability from a particular vendor implementation.

## Review triggers

- New edition, amendment, consultation draft, or corrigendum
- Regulator publication or withdrawal
- Vendor product end-of-life or security advisory
- CVE correction, exploit status change, or mitigation update
- New incident evidence or threat actor attribution update
- Material change in industrial architecture or deployment assumptions
