# OT Cybersecurity Learning Path

## Audience

Designed for IT security practitioners, network engineers, software security professionals, automation engineers, and students building OT security capability.

## Stage 1: Industrial foundations

Learn process concepts, instrumentation, PLC, RTU, DCS, SCADA, HMI, SIS, IEDs, historians, and common industrial workflows. Understand basic electrical, mechanical, process, and safety terminology.

**Practical output:** Draw a simple process and identify its sensors, controllers, actuators, operator interfaces, and safety boundaries.

## Stage 2: Industrial networking

Study Ethernet and serial communications, segmentation, routing, industrial switches, remote telemetry, time synchronization, and protocol families such as Modbus, DNP3, OPC UA, PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, BACnet, and IEC 61850.

**Practical output:** Analyze a supplied packet capture in an offline lab and document endpoints, protocol behavior, and trust assumptions.

## Stage 3: Architecture and standards

Study Purdue as a conceptual model, zones and conduits, IEC 62443, NIST SP 800-82, NIST CSF, and MITRE ATT&CK for ICS.

**Practical output:** Create an illustrative architecture, identify zones and conduits, and draft a risk-based control plan.

## Stage 4: Defensive engineering

Learn asset inventory, secure configuration, identity and remote access, vulnerability and patch management, backups, change management, supplier risk, and monitoring.

**Practical output:** Produce a safe OT hardening checklist for a simulated engineering workstation and network zone.

## Stage 5: SOC and incident response

Study passive monitoring, protocol-aware detection, alert triage, incident command, evidence handling, containment constraints, restoration, and lessons learned.

**Practical output:** Investigate synthetic alerts and prepare an OT-aware incident timeline and response recommendation.

## Stage 6: Assessment and governance

Learn authorization, rules of engagement, safety review, passive assessment, configuration review, risk analysis, compliance applicability, and evidence reporting.

**Practical output:** Complete a mock assessment report for an isolated lab with findings, impact, evidence, and remediation.

## Stage 7: Advanced research

Explore threat intelligence, industrial malware, digital twins, IIoT, cloud integration, AI agents, secure product development, and supply chain security.

**Practical output:** Create a source-backed research brief with explicit confidence, assumptions, and technical limitations.

## Recommended source starting points

- NIST OT security publications: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final
- MITRE ATT&CK for ICS: https://attack.mitre.org/matrices/ics/
- ISA/IEC 62443 series: https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-iec-62443-series-of-standards
- CISA ICS advisories: https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories
- OWASP OT: https://ot.owasp.org/

A learning path does not imply professional qualification or authorization to work on live industrial systems.
