# OT Supply Chain and Secure Lifecycle

## Scope

Industrial supply chains include control system vendors, component manufacturers, system integrators, engineering contractors, maintenance providers, cloud and remote support providers, firmware suppliers, and spare-parts channels.

## Lifecycle stages

1. Supplier selection and due diligence
2. Security and safety requirements in procurement
3. Product and component evaluation
4. Secure integration and configuration
5. Factory acceptance testing
6. Site acceptance and commissioning
7. Operations, patching, and support
8. Incident coordination and vulnerability disclosure
9. End-of-support planning
10. Decommissioning and data sanitization

## Procurement requirements to consider

- Product identity, supported versions, and lifecycle commitments
- Security development lifecycle and vulnerability handling process
- Security advisories and notification commitments
- Authentication, authorization, logging, encryption, and secure update capabilities
- Secure configuration and hardening guidance
- Software and firmware provenance
- Software bill of materials where available and appropriate
- Third-party component and dependency management
- Remote support model and access controls
- Backup, recovery, and replacement strategy
- Security testing evidence and certification claims with scope
- Incident response contacts and timelines
- Data location, retention, and access for hosted services

## Integrator and service provider assurance

Define responsibility boundaries for design, installation, commissioning, configuration, testing, documentation, remote access, maintenance, and incident support. Require secure handover artifacts such as as-built diagrams, asset lists, configuration baselines, accounts, backup procedures, and known residual risks.

## Product security claims

Do not equate a certificate, marketing claim, or component capability with secure deployment of the complete system. Record the exact product, version, certification scheme, scope, exclusions, and validity.

## End-of-life planning

Track end-of-sale, end-of-support, security patch availability, replacement lead times, compatibility, spare availability, and migration dependencies. Where replacement is not immediately feasible, define compensating segmentation, access restrictions, monitoring, backup, and risk acceptance with an expiry or review date.

## Evidence

Maintain supplier assessments, contracts, security requirements, SBOMs, product advisories, test results, commissioning records, access approvals, maintenance records, and decommissioning evidence. Protect confidential supplier and facility information.
