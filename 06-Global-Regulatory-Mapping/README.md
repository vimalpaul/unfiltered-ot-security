# Global OT Cybersecurity Regulatory Mapping

## Purpose

Create a jurisdiction-aware register of OT cybersecurity laws, regulations, regulator-issued controls, national standards, sector requirements, and official guidance.

This is not a single global compliance checklist. Applicability depends on the legal entity, sector, asset designation, service, geography, regulator, contractual obligations, and current legal instrument.

## Required record fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Jurisdiction | Country, territory, or subnational authority |
| Issuing authority | Regulator, ministry, national cybersecurity authority |
| Instrument | Exact title and identifier |
| Instrument type | Law, regulation, mandatory control, standard, policy, guidance |
| Version | Edition, revision, amendment |
| Publication date | Official date |
| Effective date | When requirements take effect |
| Applicability | Covered entities, sectors, systems, thresholds, exceptions |
| OT relevance | Explicit OT/ICS provisions or relevant general controls |
| Evidence | Official URL and access date |
| Status | Current, superseded, draft, consultation, unknown |
| Interpretation | Carefully qualified project notes |
| Review date | Actual last verification date |

## Initial jurisdiction research queue

### Middle East and GCC
- Saudi Arabia: National Cybersecurity Authority controls, including OT Cybersecurity Controls (OTCC-1:2022), and applicable sector or critical-system controls.
- United Arab Emirates: federal requirements and relevant emirate or sector requirements. Identify the responsible authority and applicability before mapping.
- Qatar: National Cyber Security Agency publications and applicable national information assurance and sector requirements.
- Bahrain: national cybersecurity authority and applicable critical infrastructure or sector requirements.
- Kuwait: national cybersecurity and sector-specific instruments, to be verified from official sources.
- Oman: national cybersecurity and sector-specific instruments, to be verified from official sources.
- Other relevant Middle Eastern jurisdictions: identify official authority and instrument before making claims.

### Asia-Pacific
India, Singapore, Australia, Japan, South Korea, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, and other relevant jurisdictions.

### Europe
European Union and member states, United Kingdom, Switzerland, and other relevant jurisdictions. Separate EU-wide legislation from national implementation and sector rules.

### North America
United States, Canada, and Mexico. Distinguish federal, state/provincial, sector, and voluntary frameworks.

### Africa and Latin America
Build country entries from official national authorities and sector regulators. Do not generalize from regional guidance.

## Initial evidence-backed records

### Saudi Arabia
The Saudi National Cybersecurity Authority publishes OT Cybersecurity Controls OTCC-1:2022. The NCA describes these controls as minimum cybersecurity requirements for OT systems and as an extension to its Essential Cybersecurity Controls. The NCA also publishes an OTCC implementation guide and related templates. Applicability must be determined from the official instrument and the organization's status.

Official source: https://nca.gov.sa/en/regulatory-documents/controls-list/otcc/

### United States
NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 is published guidance for OT security, not a law by itself. NIST's Rev. 4 initial public draft was published on 21 September 2026; it remains a draft and must be labeled accordingly.

Official sources:
- https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final
- https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r4/ipd

## Important distinctions

- A national strategy is not automatically a binding control standard.
- A regulator's guidance is not automatically a legal obligation.
- A standard may become mandatory through law, regulation, licensing, contract, or regulator direction.
- Critical infrastructure designation may change which requirements apply.
- A control crosswalk does not prove compliance.
- A published document may have later amendments, implementation deadlines, or superseding instruments.

## Research workflow

For every entry, capture the primary document, legal basis, covered entity definition, sectors, effective date, enforcement model, exemptions, related OT clauses, and status. If these cannot be confirmed, label the entry "Research required" rather than guessing.
