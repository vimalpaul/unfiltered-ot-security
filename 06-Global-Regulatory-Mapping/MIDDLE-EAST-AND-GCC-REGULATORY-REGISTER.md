# Middle East and GCC OT Cybersecurity Regulatory Register

## Purpose and limitations

This register is an evidence-led starting point for research. It is not legal advice and does not determine whether a particular organization or facility is in scope. Applicability must be checked against the official instrument, legal basis, entity definition, sector, asset designation, contract, and current amendments.

The register deliberately separates OT-specific instruments from broader information assurance requirements that may apply to organizations holding OT-related information assets.

## Required review before publication

For each instrument, verify:
1. Official issuer and canonical publication.
2. Exact title, identifier, edition, amendments, and current status.
3. Legal basis and whether the instrument is mandatory, advisory, contractual, or voluntary.
4. Covered entities, ownership types, sectors, asset classes, and geographic scope.
5. Effective dates, transition periods, exemptions, and enforcement.
6. OT/ICS clauses and whether they are explicit or inferred from general information security requirements.
7. Related implementation guides, assessment tools, and official FAQs.
8. Source access date and reviewer.

## Evidence notes

- Saudi NCA's OTCC page identifies OTCC-1:2022 and describes it as minimum cybersecurity requirements for OT systems and an extension to NCA ECC.
- Qatar NCSA's NIA v2.1 document describes a national information assurance standard for organizations and their information assets. NCSA's certification page describes the certification process. Do not relabel NIA as an OT-specific standard.
- DESC's standards page describes the DESC ICS Standard as applicable to Dubai government and semi-government entities that operate critical infrastructure and/or ICS/OT. This is a Dubai-specific statement, not a UAE-wide conclusion.
